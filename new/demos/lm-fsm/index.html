Lockheed Martin autopilot: the requirements in conflict, and the time the pullup takes#

The system. An autopilot of an aircraft commands a safety maneuver, a pullup, when a sensor reports that the flight limits are exceeded. The requirements come from the Ten Lockheed Martin Cyber-Physical Challenges (Mavridou et al., RE 2020), first as written and then corrected, as in the case study LMCPS of FRET, the requirements tool of NASA. Each requirement is a transition of a state machine, written in LTL (x' is x at the next step). Autopilot has the states STATE 0 TRANSITION, 1 NOMINAL, 2 MANEUVER (the pullup), 3 STANDBY; it reads standby (the pilot is in control), apfail (an external failure), supported (the system is healthy), and good (the sensor sees no fault). Sensor has the states SENSTATE 0 NOMINAL, 1 TRANSITION, 2 FAULT; it reads limits (the limits are exceeded) and the mode of the autopilot. The contracts assume nothing of the environment. slugs checks realizability, nuXmv the refinements.

The question. Two questions. Which of the first requirements are in conflict, so that no autopilot can meet them? And once they are corrected, does the pullup come as soon as the system requirement says? The first is asked before anyone builds the autopilot, the second before anyone signs off its timing.

The system: the autopilot and the sensor, inside the system requirement, FSM-001.

The steps#

The tags on the boxes give the results of the checks: green holds, red fails; Diagrams says how to read them.

Step 1. The first requirements. A contract is realizable when some state machine meets it whatever the environment does. The first requirements of the autopilot are not. CHASE finds an unrealizable core, the smallest set of requirements that no machine meets together: the initial state, G ((STATE = 0 & standby') -> STATE' = 3), and G ((STATE = 0 & supported' & good) -> STATE' = 1). In TRANSITION, with the pilot in control and good data, one says STANDBY and the other NOMINAL. The script replaces one requirement per core by its corrected version until the contract is realizable: four conflicts in the autopilot, one in the sensor, the ones FRET reports. Finding them by reading 14 requirements is hard; each core points at two to four.

Step 1: the first requirements; the cores in red.

Step 2. The system. The corrected Autopilot and Sensor are realizable. Composed, they do not refine Pullup, G ((limits & !standby & !apfail & supported) -> pullup): a pullup at the step the limits are exceeded. In the counterexample, the limits are exceeded at step 1, but the autopilot is still in NOMINAL.

Step 2: the composition does not refine Pullup; the values at step 1.

Step 3. The system, with time. The sensor and the autopilot each take a step to react. PullupTimed gives the pullup that time: after three steps within the limits and one beyond, with the pilot not in control and no failure, the pullup comes at the fifth step. The composition refines it.

Step 3: the composition refines PullupTimed.

Run it#

pip install chase-cps
chase-cps install nuxmv --accept-license
chase-cps install slugs
python fsm.py --expect

Contracts in C: each function checked against the clauses in its file#

The system. Small C functions of controllers: the pump of a water tank (tank.c), a ring buffer (ring.c), and a limit on the climb rate of a drone (climb.c). Their contracts are written in the C file itself, as the C checkers ESBMC, CBMC, and Frama-C read them: requires clauses say what a call may assume (the assumptions of the contract), ensures clauses what the function must give back (its guarantees). CHASE reads these clauses, in any of the three syntaxes, and draws each function as a component whose inputs are its parameters and whose output is its return value. ESBMC (or CBMC) checks one call of the function against its contract. The clauses CHASE reads are listed on the page C programs.

The question. Does each function meet the contract written next to it, and what does a failure look like? Code with no contract at all: can it at least be checked for overflow? The same file then serves the C checker and the contracts of a whole system.

The system of tank.c: pump, with the terms read from its clauses, and flow, with none.

The steps#

Step 1. The pump meets its contract. pump (clauses of ESBMC) runs at full speed (3) from a level of 80 and is off below 20. ESBMC proves that every call within the assumed levels, 0 to 100, gives back what the contract asks, in about 0.1 s (the tag M ⊨ C).

Step 1: pump meets the contract read from its clauses.

Step 2. Code with no clause. flow divides the liters by the seconds. With no contract, it is checked for overflow, division by zero, and failed asserts (the tag safe). liters / seconds overflows for liters = -2147483648, seconds = -1; the panel gives the line.

Step 2: flow is not safe code.

Step 3. The ring buffer, with and without the wrap. advance (clauses of CBMC) gives the next slot of a ring buffer of 8 slots, and meets its contract. advance_fast does not wrap at the end: the slot i = 7 gives ni = 8, and the term ni <= 7 is red. The failing call is on the ports, ready to replay.

Step 3: advance meets its contract, advance_fast does not.

Step 4. A contract in ACSL. climb.c limits the climb rate of the drone of the page Drone climb to 3, with its contract in an ACSL comment, /*@ ... */, the language of Frama-C. limit meets it (ESBMC checks it; Frama-C is not run). limit_loose tests the top with v > 4, so v = 4 gives 4.

Step 4: limit meets its ACSL contract, limit_loose does not.

Once read, a function is a contract like any other: it can be composed with the others and checked against the contract of a system, as in Fire-suppression drones in C. The tags of the pictures are explained on the page Diagrams.

Run it#

pip install "chase-cps[z3]"      # and ESBMC or CBMC
python contractsinc.py --expect

The files of the demo are in the repository; Running the demos says what the scripts print and how to use the diagrams.

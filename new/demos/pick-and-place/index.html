Pick-and-place arm: the mission refines the target, and the coordinator is synthesized#

The system. A robotic arm picks a piece, turns, and places it at a target position. The example is the second part of a tutorial at the DATE 2021 University Booth; the first part is the aircraft power system. specs/robotic_arm.ltl has seven contracts in LTL over Booleans and small integers:

  • Target, the requirement: request a pick (rpick) until the piece is picked, then a place (rplace);
  • Mission, the design of the mission planner: the same requests, and also the target position (xtarget, ytarget) and the angle (desired_angle) for each phase;
  • Coordinator: commands the pick and the place (cmd_pick, cmd_place) once the arm is in position and turned;
  • Move, Turn, Pick, Place: the four actions of the arm, which report pdone, adone, picked, placed.

nuXmv checks the contracts and slugs synthesizes controllers. The steps use the console of logics_tool, the command-line tool of CHASE.

The question. Does the mission meet the requirement? Do the seven contracts fit together, that is, can their assumptions and guarantees all hold? And can the coordinator be built from its contract? These are the checks to run before writing any code for the arm.

flowchart LR Mission -- "rpick, rplace" --> Coordinator Mission -- "xtarget, ytarget" --> Move Mission -- "desired_angle" --> Turn Move -- pdone --> Coordinator Turn -- adone --> Coordinator Coordinator -- cmd_pick --> Pick Coordinator -- cmd_place --> Place Pick -- "picked, grip" --> Coordinator Place -- placed --> Coordinator Pick -- picked --> Mission Place -- placed --> Mission
The system: the mission planner, the coordinator, and the four actions, with the signals between them.

The steps#

Step 1. The mission refines the target. Mission refines Target: it assumes no more and guarantees at least as much, for every behavior. The console runs the commands of solution.script and writes the checks for nuXmv, which finds both formulas true:

$> logics_tool -i specs/robotic_arm.ltl -o /tmp/ -c solution.script
$> nuXmv /tmp/MT.smv

Step 1: Mission refines Target (⪯ green).

Step 2. The other direction. The reverse check shows that Mission says more than Target, so the design is a true refinement and not a copy of the requirement: Target does not refine Mission. In /tmp/TM.smv the formula of the guarantees is false. In the counterexample the piece is picked at step 1, and at step 2 xtarget, ytarget, and desired_angle are still 0: Target says nothing about them.

Step 2: Target does not refine Mission; the counterexample at step 2.

Step 3. The whole system. compose builds the system of the seven contracts, and verify writes the negation of its assumptions, of its guarantees, and of both. nuXmv -bmc /tmp/system.smv finds a run for each: the system is compatible (some environment meets its assumptions) and consistent (the parts can meet their guarantees).

Step 4. The coordinator. synthesize Coordinator coordinator slugs writes the coordinator for slugs, which finds it realizable (a controller exists for every behavior of the arm and the mission) and synthesizes one of 128 states. By hand this controller would be written and then tested; here it comes from its contract.

The tags of the pictures are explained on the page Diagrams.

Run it#

pip install chase-cps
chase-cps install nuxmv --accept-license
chase-cps install slugs
sh diagram.sh

The files of the demo are in the repository; Running the demos says what the scripts print and how to use the diagrams.

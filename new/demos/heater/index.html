Room thermostat: repairing the threshold, then checking its C code#

The system. A thermostat keeps a room warm without overheating it. It is a small example made for these pages. Room starts at 60 degrees and, at each step, gains the heat u and loses 1. Thermostat heats (u = 3) while the temperature T is below a threshold theta, here 76; it is also written in C, in heater.c. The requirement Comfort asks T <= tmax (75 degrees) at every step. The contracts are written in signal temporal logic in discrete time over 10 steps (G[0,10] is "at each of the steps 0 to 10"), so the solver z3 checks them.

The question. Does the thermostat keep the room within 75 degrees? If not, which threshold does, and does the C code implement it? The designer needs a threshold that works before tuning the code.

The system: Room and Thermostat (with its C code) inside the requirement Comfort.

The steps#

Step 1. The thermostat overshoots. Room and Thermostat composed do not refine Comfort (refine: every behavior of the parts meets the requirement). The counterexample: the room heats to 76, cools to 75, and heats again to 77 at step 10. CHASE also computes the robustness of T <= 75, the margin by which it holds in the worst case: -2, two degrees too hot.

Steps 1 and 2: the counterexample at the top (the slider puts step 8 on the ports), the two repairs at the bottom.

Step 2. Repair. repair changes a named constant as little as possible so that the check passes, and shows that no smaller change works. On the thermostat it gives theta = 74, a change of 2: the room then reaches 75 and no more. On the requirement it gives tmax = 77: the requirement accepts the overshoot. In the viewer, tmax belongs to the requirement and is picked only by hand.

Step 3. The code. Each step of Thermostat is one call of the C function, so ESBMC checks one call against one step of the formula (the tag M ⊨ C). With theta = 76 it fails: the call with T = 75 returns 0 where the contract asks for 3. repair finds the threshold that the code implements: 74.

Step 3: M ⊨ C fails on T = 75.

Step 4. Between the samples. A step is a sample taken every 20 s, and the temperature changes at most 0.1 degree per second (its Lipschitz constant: a bound on how fast it changes). Between two samples it can then move by 2 degrees, so CHASE checks T + 2 < 75 at the samples (Fainekos and Pappas): if that holds, T <= 75 holds at every instant. With theta = 74 the samples reach 75: the answer is unknown, not a counterexample, since the margin is only sufficient. With theta = 71 the refinement holds in continuous time.

$> chase-cps check heater.ltl --period 20 --lipschitz T=0.1 --refines Room+Thermostat Comfort

The tags of the pictures are explained on the page Diagrams.

Run it#

pip install "chase-cps[z3]"      # and ESBMC or CBMC for step 4
python heater.py --expect
chase-cps view heater.ltl --check --serve

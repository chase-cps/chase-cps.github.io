NASA Lift-Plus-Cruise aircraft: what the pilot must do to hover in time#

The system. Lift-Plus-Cruise is a NASA concept of an electric aircraft that takes off and hovers on lift rotors and cruises on a wing. Its mode logic sets lift_mode: 3 wing borne (flying on the wing), 2 semi wing borne, 1 semi thrust borne, 0 thrust borne (hovering on the rotors). It reads the airspeed kias and the ground speed kgs (knots), and the hover control hover_control_mode, which the pilot turns on. The requirements come from the LPC_mini example of FRET, the requirements tool of NASA (Pressburger et al., REFSQ 2023), as LTL formulas over real numbers, one formula per requirement. The vehicle is split in two parts: ModeLogic, with the requirements on the modes, and Pilot, which sets the speeds and the hover control: it starts at 120 knots and changes the speed by at most 10 per step. The checks run on nuXmv, with its SMT engine for the reals.

The question. The requirements say what the mode logic does, not what the pilot does, so some flights hover within 12 steps and others never do. What must the pilot do so that every flight hovers in time? A designer of the pilot, or of a flight procedure, needs exactly this contract.

The system: the pilot drives the mode logic, inside the requirements with the deadline of 12 steps.

The steps#

The tags on the boxes give the results of the checks: green holds, red fails; Diagrams says how to read them.

Step 1. Two deadlines. With a deadline of 12 steps the requirements are consistent: some flight meets them all. With 6 they are not, and CHASE finds the unsat core, the smallest set of requirements in conflict: 8 of 18. They say why: kias starts at 120 and changes by at most 10 per step, the vehicle stays wing borne above 90 and semi wing borne above 30, so it cannot hover by step 6.

Step 1: consistent with 12 steps, not with 6; the core in red.

Step 2. The split. ModeLogic ⊗ Pilot (their composition) refines Vehicle, the requirements without a deadline.

Step 2: the split refines the vehicle.

Step 3. What the pilot must do. CHASE computes the quotient Q12 = Reach12 / ModeLogic, the dashed box: the weakest contract of the pilot with which every flight hovers within 12 steps. It is derived from the requirements, not written by hand. Pilot, with the requirements alone, does not refine it: in the counterexample kias stays at 120 and the vehicle never leaves the wing.

Step 3: Pilot does not refine Q12: kias stays at 120.

Step 4. A pilot that slows down. Slow lowers kias by 10 per step, but never turns the hover control on: the vehicle stops at semi thrust borne, and Slow does not refine Q12.

Step 4: Slow slows down, but never turns the hover control on.

Step 5. A pilot that turns on the hover control. Decel also turns the hover control on when kgs is below 20, and refines Q12: with the mode logic, every flight hovers within 12 steps. It does not refine Q06 = Reach06 / ModeLogic: no pilot can, as step 1 showed.

Step 5: Decel refines Q12.

Step 5: Decel does not refine Q06: at step 6 the vehicle is still semi wing borne.

python lpc.py --expect

Aircraft power system: the bounds as written conflict, relaxed they are realizable#

The system. The electric power system of an aircraft, from Nuzzo et al., "CHASE: Contract-based requirement engineering for cyber-physical system design" (DATE 2018). A left and a right generator each feed an AC bus, a rectifier, a DC bus, and a load. Contactors (switches) connect each generator to its AC bus (contactors 1 and 2), and the two AC buses to each other (contactor 3). A controller opens and closes the contactors; it reads the state of the generators and of the contactors, and commands the contactors. The system and its requirements are written in the language of CHASE for power systems (read by load_dsl), which turns them into a GR(1) contract for the controller (GR(1) is the fragment of LTL that a synthesis tool such as slugs solves): it assumes that a contactor switches in 10 ms or less and that at most one generator fails, and guarantees, among others, that the generators never run in parallel and that no DC bus stays without power more than 15 ms. Times become counters: a step is 5 ms.

The question. Can the contactors be switched so that the requirements hold whatever fails? If not, which requirements are in conflict? The answer tells the system engineers which bound to renegotiate before anyone designs the controller.

The system: the controller of the contactors, with its inputs and outputs.

The steps#

The tags on the boxes give the results of the checks: green holds, red fails; Diagrams says how to read them.

Step 1. The requirements as written. The contract (11 inputs, 7 outputs, 30 assumptions, 28 guarantees) is not realizable: no controller meets it whatever the generators do. CHASE finds the unrealizable core, 4 of the 28 guarantees: contactors 1, 2, and 3 are never all closed, and the three terms of the counter of one DC bus allow it at most 15 ms without power. When a generator fails, its DC bus can be fed again only through contactor 3, after the contactor of that generator opens: two switchings of up to 10 ms each, more than 15 ms.

Step 1: the requirements as written are unrealizable; the terms of the core are red.

Step 2. The relaxed requirements. template_0_realizable.txt changes two lines: 20 ms to disconnect a failed generator, 30 ms for a DC bus (a step is then 10 ms). The contract (24 assumptions, 26 guarantees) is realizable, and slugs synthesizes a controller of 97 states from it.

Step 2: the relaxed requirements are realizable (R).

The same language describes a factory.

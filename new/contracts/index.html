Contract theory#

An assume-guarantee contract specifies a component by what it assumes of its environment and what it guarantees in return. The theory of contracts says how such specifications are composed, compared, and divided, independently of the logic in which they are written; CHASE writes them in linear temporal logic.

Contracts and behaviors#

A behavior is a sequence of values of the variables, the inputs x and the outputs y. A formula denotes a set of behaviors. A contract C = (A, G) is a pair of such sets: the assumptions A and the guarantees G. Two contracts with the same A and the same A → G (the saturated guarantees) have the same implementations and environments, so CHASE treats them as equal.

Notion Holds when Meaning
M is an implementation of C (M ⊨M C) M ∧ A → G in every environment that satisfies A, M satisfies G
E is an environment of C (E ⊨E C) E → A E behaves as the contract assumes
C is compatible A is satisfiable C has an environment
C is consistent A → G is satisfiable C has an implementation
C1 refines C2 (C1 ⪯ C2) A2 → A1 and (A1 → G1) → (A2 → G2) C1 can replace C2: it assumes less and guarantees more

Refinement is what makes design by contracts compositional: if C1 refines C2, every implementation of C1 is an implementation of C2, and every environment of C2 is an environment of C1. A component designed for C1 can take the place of one designed for C2, without looking at the rest of the system.

Building contracts from contracts#

The composition C1 ∥ C2 is the contract of two components placed side by side, with the variables of the same name connected: it guarantees G1 ∧ G2, under the assumptions that the environment meets both A1 and A2, unless the components themselves violate them. If M1 implements C1 and M2 implements C2, then M1 ∥ M2 implements C1 ∥ C2. The other operations answer other design questions:

  • The quotient C / C1: what must the missing component of a system satisfy?
  • The conjunction C1 ∧ C2: what satisfies two specifications at once?
  • The merging C1 • C2: what are two viewpoints of the same component, together?
  • The separation, the implication, and the coimplication are the adjoints of merging, conjunction, and disjunction.

Contract algebra gives their definitions and sources.

In CHASE#

  • Formulas: the assumptions and the guarantees are formulas of linear temporal logic over Boolean, integer, real, and bit-vector variables, written in Python, in the CHASE specification language, or in SystemVerilog Assertions.
  • Contract algebra: the operations, computed in closed form or keeping the terms of the contracts.
  • Contract-based verification: the notions of the table above, each reduced to the validity or the satisfiability of a formula, which a model checker decides, with counterexamples and cores.

For contracts in the GR(1) fragment of LTL, GR(1) synthesis asks whether a component can be synthesized from its contract.

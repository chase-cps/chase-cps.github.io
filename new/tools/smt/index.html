SMT and optimization (z3)#

This page is for readers whose contracts compare numbers: budgets, sizings, timing bounds, signals over a bounded horizon. CHASE gives such questions, and every optimization, to the SMT solver z3, installed with the package:

$> pip install "chase-cps[z3]"

Checks of formulas over a bounded number of steps#

A formula with no G, F, U, or R reads a bounded number of steps: X, x', past(x, n), and intervals such as G[0,10] p or F[1,3] p. A contract made of such formulas is a static contract (one step) or a contract of signal temporal logic in discrete time. Its checks are questions of arithmetic over the values of the variables at each step, which z3 answers exactly for linear arithmetic over integers and reals, with any Boolean structure.

With z3 installed, CHASE sends these formulas to z3 by itself, in every check of contract-based verification, and the result names the back end z3. Formulas with G, F, U, or R over the whole behavior go to the model checker, and so do words (bit vectors).

Optimization: the problems of a MILP#

The optimization questions of CHASE are linear problems over integers, reals, and Boolean structure, the problems that a mixed-integer linear program (MILP) solves:

  • minimize and maximize: the best value of a term where the constraints of a contract hold, and the values that reach it (formulas over a bounded number of steps);
  • the worst case of a formula under constraints, its robustness, as for the latency bounds of ContrACT in the Drone timing demo;
  • the values of named constants at the least cost, in repair.

z3 solves them with its optimization engine (νZ: Bjørner, Phan, and Fleckenstein, TACAS 2015). It computes with rational numbers, so the optimum is exact and a strict inequality is kept as it is (an optimum that is not reached is reported as such); a disjunction or an implication is written as it is, with no big-M constant; and the variables need no bounds. Each optimum is checked again by the solver before it is given.

Dedicated MILP solvers (SCIP, HiGHS) are planned for problems with thousands of variables, with z3 checking their answers.

Running another solver#

chase-cps export FILE --to smt writes a check as SMT-LIB 2, for z3, cvc5, or another solver you run yourself (command line). When the checks run with NuSMV, which has no real type, z3 also refines the abstraction of the comparisons of reals (real variables).

The demos Drone climb, Room thermostat, and Drone timing are checked and optimized by z3.
